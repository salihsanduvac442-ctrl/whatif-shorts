# What if a submarine never stopped diving? — Treatment

Style: Isometric Infographic (`iso-infographic`). 1080×1920 vertical Short, 24 fps, ~42 s, English voice (Kokoro), burned-in map-label subtitles.

## Three candidate structures
1. **One continuous tilt down through strata** — a tall isometric water-column board; the camera rides the submarine down while the board's front face is the strata. Milestones arrive as label pins. Open ending: the sub breaks through the sea floor into rock, past the deepest hole ever drilled, and the route line keeps going into an empty plate with "?".
2. **Two boards compared** — the sub next to a skyscraper stack measuring the same depth. Clear scale, but two subjects split attention on a phone screen.
3. **Countdown of depth records** — list of record holders (diver, whale, wreck, fish) each on its own small board, cut between. Faster, but loses the "never stops" feeling that made the digging video work.

**Chosen: 1.** It is the escalation format the channel performs best with, and the style's "top-to-bottom tilt through strata" + "tracked subject" moves do exactly that.

## Logline and arc
A small yellow submarine starts at a sunny sea surface and keeps going down. Setup: surface, divers' limit. Turn: light dies, pressure climbs, life gets stranger. Peak: the bottom of the ocean — and it doesn't stop; it drills on into rock. Ending: an unanswered station, "?".

## Benchmark
- *Powers of Ten*: a continuous, steady descent with a scale counter — learn: one unbroken move, numbers as the clock. Don't take: its framing or titles.
- Kurzgesagt depth explainers — learn: one visual metaphor per step, colour codes depth. Don't take: characters, palette, music.

## Shot list (one take, camera = world centre + log zoom)
| # | Time | Framing | Move | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3.5 | Wide: whole board top, title in iso type on the water | Hold, then push in to the sub | Hook: question + subject in 3 s |
| 2 | 3.5–30 | Medium on sub, ≥1/3 frame height of water column around it | Tracked tilt down, speed rising section by section | Escalation; depth = time |
| 3 | per milestone | Label pin + circular inset (whale, wreck, fish) | Leader grows → inset opens | Frame within frame, subject stays clear |
| 4 | 30–34 | Sea floor | Brief hold + shake, then cut line draws on the floor | Turn: "but it doesn't stop" |
| 5 | 34–39 | Rock strata | Drill through, fast | Acceleration |
| 6 | 39–42 | Fast log pull-out to show the whole column; route ends at "?" plate | Pull-out | Scale reveal + open ending |

## Beat sheet / cue map
Tempo 120 bpm (beat 0.5 s). Each milestone lands on a bar line; the voice line starts with the pin. Low-pass on the music closes with depth; instruments drop out (marimba → pizzicato → bass pulse only). Two near-silences: at the sea floor (31 s) and just before the "?" (40 s).

## Sound design
| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface | soft surf, gulls (very low) | splash on dive | marimba + shaker, bright |
| Twilight–midnight | low water hum | faint sonar ping per milestone | pizzicato, low-pass closing |
| Hadal | deep rumble | hull creak (quiet) | bass pulse only |
| Floor | near-silence | thud on contact | — |
| Rock | drill grind (low) | rock crack | pulse returns, faster |
Narrator loud and steady in front for every line; effects barely audible (channel rule).

## Subtitles & titles
Map-label cards bottom-centre (pale card, ink border, iso cube chip), Jost-style geometric sans. Big depth counter top-left as an Isotype-style HUD with a pressure readout. Nothing covers the submarine.
